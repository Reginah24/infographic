The death gap is closing, fast

Death registration fell to 26.2% in 2021, then climbed every year after. It reached 50.5% in 2025, up 19.1 points since 2019. Births stayed above 84% the whole time.

Who is still missing from the record?

In 2025, about 38,500 expected deaths were not registered. The gap is biggest where deaths happen away from hospitals, and for women.

What is working: asking families, and doctors writing it down

When someone dies at home, a trained interviewer can ask the family about the symptoms and work out the likely cause. This is a verbal autopsy. Rwanda now does about 10 times more of them than in 2022.

Why every death record matters

A registered death with a cause tells health planners what is killing people. The deaths counted in 2025 show a clear pattern.

What the records reveal

Heart disease

is the leading cause of death at home: cardiac diseases take the top two places (16.2% and 11.1% of community deaths).

Newborns

Conditions around birth are the #1 cause in health facilities (14.9%). The neonatal death rate fell from 23.5 to 18.7 per 1,000 live births between 2019 and 2025. ↓ 4.8

Non-communicable diseases like heart disease, cancer, stroke and diabetes now cause about half of all recorded deaths. We only know this because those deaths were counted.

Sources: Figure 25, Table 17, Table 10.8 (adjusted neonatal mortality rate).

What can close the gap

Count every life, from first breath to last

Births show it can be done: 99.1% of registered births in 2025 were registered on time. Deaths can follow.

  1. FamiliesRegister every death within 30 days, especially when it happens at home.
  2. CommunitiesGrow verbal autopsy coverage in rural districts, where most deaths happen at home.
  3. Policy makersUse cause-of-death data to target heart disease and newborn care.
